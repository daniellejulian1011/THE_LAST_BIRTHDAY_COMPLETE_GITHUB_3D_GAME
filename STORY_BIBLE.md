# THE LAST BIRTHDAY — STORY BIBLE
## Locked narrative edition · October 27, 1983

**Genre:** First-person survival horror / psychological mystery / choice-driven adventure.
**Setting:** Candlewick's Celebration Palace, Hollowbrook, Maine. Opened 1968; permanently abandoned after the fire of October 27, 1973.
**Playable protagonist:** June Mercer, aged 24 in 1983; aged 14 in 1973.
**Tagline:** Some parties never end.

## The premise

Ten years after her seven-year-old brother Robin died in the fire at Candlewick's Celebration Palace, June Mercer receives an unmarked cassette from the final birthday celebration. It contains Mr. Morrow's cheerful 1973 recorded greeting, the fire alarm, and three knocks: the private signal Robin used with June. She returns to the condemned building to learn what owner Edwin Candlewick concealed about Robin's last moments. The halls are no longer empty. Mr. Morrow, an enormous deteriorated pink rabbit mascot, moves on his own although his internal speaking system was destroyed ten years earlier.

The horror is built around **an entirely preventable human catastrophe and an unexplained supernatural aftermath**. No voice researchers, scientific experimentation, or secret labs exist in the story.

## What happened in 1973? The truth

1. During the afternoon and evening of October 27, Robin celebrated his seventh birthday with ten other children. The original speaking Mr. Morrow greeted guests using his prerecorded performer voice and songs, just like classic children's party entertainment.
2. Maintenance supervisor Ruth Hale had repeatedly filed reports about overheated stage electrical wiring and dangerous backstage exits.
3. The owner, Edwin Candlewick, ignored repairs and ordered emergency backstage doors chained against unauthorized entry.
4. Around 8:43 p.m., the overloaded stage circuit sparked and ignited the curtains. Smoke filled the party room.
5. Ten children escaped. Robin helped Ivy Bell reach a narrow service opening, then became trapped deeper backstage. He used his three-knock signal to try to alert June.
6. Edwin heard the knocks and had access to the key, but did not open the door. His guilt is documented in the manager's ledger: **“I heard him knock. I did not open the door.”**
7. Robin **died in the fire at age seven**. The remains were not recovered. This is a confirmed part of the story: he is not alive or secretly hiding in 1983.
8. The business shut down permanently because of the fatal fire, chained exits, and safety violations. Edwin disappeared during the investigation.
9. Ruth later testified that Mr. Morrow's damaged costume turned its head **after** power had been cut. No one could explain why.

## Cast and motivations

### June Mercer — protagonist
- **1973:** 14. Witness to the celebration and ensuing emergency; Robin's protective older sister.
- **1983:** 24. Amateur photographer, emotionally guarded, investigative, loyal, and persistent.
- **Visuals:** Dark brown ponytail and bangs, rust red bomber-style jacket, light striped shirt, faded denim, work boots, canvas backpack, camera, flashlight.
- **Objective:** Establish who failed Robin and why those failures were concealed. She does not believe he is alive.
- **Conflict:** Traumatic memories and survivor guilt, versus the evidence that adult decisions—not June—caused the tragedy.

### Robin Mercer — deceased child
- **1973:** 7, beloved brother, fond of the pink rabbit mascot and birthday performances.
- **1983:** Has been dead for ten years. Appears **only** in 1973 memories, archived photos, and recordings that do not rewrite his death.
- **Signature:** Three knocks that only June understands.
- **Conflict:** His last actions helped another child escape; his death is the human heart of the mystery.

### Mr. Morrow — mascot / principal threat
- **1968–1973:** Pink-and-cream rabbit with blue vest, red bow tie, large expressive eyes, white gloves and oversized shoes. Cheerful **recorded** performer greetings and songs were part of stage shows and walk-around entertainment. Employees operated the costume. His voice is heard in **1973 archival tapes only** during the 1983 chapters.
- **1983:** Blackened, grimy fabric, torn ears, cracked cream mask, hollow eye sockets, exposed harness, rusted oversized shoes. Wanders without a visible wearer or explanation.
- **Unbreakable rule:** The 1983 figure is **COMPLETELY MUTE**: no speech, whispers, laughter, screaming, mimicry or lines of dialogue. Sounds limited to shoes, fabric, joints, mechanisms, impact. Voices from old audio must always originate from separate cassette players or PA speakers.
- **Behavior:** Stare, head tilt, wave, slowly approach, pursue, seize and seat victims at the forgotten table.
- **Driving impulse:** Repeat or complete Robin's interrupted party. Do not definitively reveal that Robin is inside the mascot.

### Edwin Candlewick — human source of wrongdoing
- **1973:** About 49. Founder and owner; charismatic, refined, controlling businessman in checked suit, burgundy waistcoat and tie, signet ring.
- **Goals:** Protect revenue, reputation, and control; chose to ignore Ruth's warnings.
- **1973 secret:** Ordered doors chained and failed to open the backstage exit despite hearing Robin knock.
- **1983:** Missing since the inquiry. Appears through historical photographs, documents, flashbacks, and his office records; final fate left unresolved.

### Ruth Hale — ignored maintenance supervisor
- **1973:** About 41. Practical, ethical, blunt, knowledgeable technician with olive jacket, navy work clothes, flashlight, keys, radio and tool belt.
- **Goals:** Repair the electrical system and prevent an accident. Told management the stage wiring was dangerous.
- **1983 IMPORTANT RULE:** Ruth **DOES NOT PHYSICALLY APPEAR IN 1983**. Her role is found in archived testimony, the maintenance log, 1973 flashbacks and photos.
- **Supernatural clue:** Reported the ruined costume moving with the electrical supply off.

### Ivy Bell — survivor
- **1973:** A young birthday guest rescued via the service corridor by Robin.
- **1983:** Survivor whose testimony helps reconstruct Robin's final minutes; no requirement she appear physically in the playable present.
- **Function:** Provides evidence of Robin's bravery without contradicting his death.

## Building and atmosphere

**Entrance Lobby** — Rain leaking through the boarded roof. Mildewed posters and faded check-in desk. The player can walk back out and take the unresolved escape ending.

**Party Room** — Eleven place settings and a table once prepared for Robin. Cassette 01. A photograph triggers a **1973 animated memory reconstruction** containing younger June, Robin, Edwin, Ruth, Ivy, and friendly Mr. Morrow.

**Arcade** — Rows of black CRT arcade cabinets, rusted token machines and a metal prize locker for temporary hiding. Ruth's 1973 testimony cassette is here.

**Manager's Office** — Filing cabinets, brass keys and Edwin's ledger. Reveals why the exits were chained and what happened to Robin.

**Maintenance** — Generator, missing ceramic fuse, overloaded electrical hardware and Ruth's old warnings. Restoring power activates the old speaker loop and wakes the supernatural rabbit.

**Back Hall** — Long corridor with fire-blackened walls, a chained backstage door and intermittent three knocks. A key and the evidence allow access.

**Birthday Stage** — Broken curtains, final chair, original incident report and the last choice. The 1983 rabbit waits mute.

## Gameplay chapters

**Prologue — The Tape:** Opening overlay with two choices about how June prepares to enter the palace. No suggestion that Robin's speaking voice is alive in 1983.

**Chapter 1 — The Entrance:** Exploration, first collectible envelope, the option to leave.

**Chapter 2 — Empty Party Rooms:** Collect historical show cassette; discover the 1973 family photograph and animated flashback.

**Chapter 3 — The Workers' Warning:** Investigate the arcade and manager's office, find Ruth's testimony and Edwin's ledger.

**Chapter 4 — The Rabbit Wakes:** Find the fuse, restore power, hear old PA music; stealth, pursuit, hiding and a limited camera-flash stun mechanic.

**Chapter 5 — The Locked Door:** Unlock backstage, reconcile Robin's three knocks with Edwin's confession.

**Final Act — The Last Birthday:** Confront the truth and choose the ending.

## The six endings

1. **THE LAST SILENCE:** June retrieves the signed evidence by answering the hatch with three knocks; the cover-up is exposed.
2. **THE NEW HOST:** June sits at the party table; Mr. Morrow makes her another permanent guest.
3. **ASHES & CONFETTI:** June overloads the ruined system and escapes a second fire with the evidence; the mascot's fate remains uncertain.
4. **THE ELEVENTH GUEST:** June follows the knocks into an impossible chamber and disappears.
5. **THE GUEST WHO LEFT:** June returns to her car and escapes but does not resolve the mystery.
6. **NEVER FOUND:** Mr. Morrow catches June repeatedly; a game-over ending.

## Horror direction

- **Silence before jumpscares.** Mr. Morrow must never provide a vocal warning.
- **Uncanny hospitality.** A gesture made in an old cheerful 1973 photo is repeated by the damaged 1983 rabbit.
- **Environmental clues.** Chains, burns, warnings and records all tell the human part of the story.
- **Trauma without retcon.** Robin's death remains certain; June's visions and knocked signals are supernatural, not proof of survival.
- **Unresolved supernatural origin.** Explanations must not erase human negligence or replace it with scientific experimentation.

## Audio layers

- **1983 space:** dark electrical drone, dripping water, faint wind, CRT hum and distant loudspeaker pops.
- **1973 evidence only:** old cheerful prerecorded Mr. Morrow greetings and birthday tunes, marred by analog hiss and the fire alarm.
- **1983 Mr. Morrow:** footstep thunks, rusty servos, textured fabric scrape, an occasional jaw click. Absolutely no vocal sound.
- **Climax:** three short knocks followed by the noise of the stage control equipment.

## Deliverable and production status

This GitHub package contains a **playable, stylized 3D browser prototype**, not a finished AAA production. The eight GLB character files are actual skinned meshes with 15-joint hierarchies and named animation clips. They are low-detail procedural prototype art assets, not the high-resolution sculpts in the supplied concept images. They can be inspected and animated in the included `model-viewer.html` and replaced with higher fidelity GLBs without rewriting the story.
